# Market definition (Fred & Barney)

Williams illustrates his definition of a market with a Flintstones-style parable: Fred trades dinosaur meat for Barney's club. A market, in his framing, is nothing more than a mechanism that finds the point where buyers and sellers *agree on price* while still *disagreeing on value* — if everyone agreed on value there'd be no trade at all.[^practical-fractal-video]

## Consequences he draws from this

- **Bullish/bearish consensus is meaningless.** A "75% bullish" reading just means you haven't polled the rest of the bears yet — if a true consensus existed, price would already reflect it and the trade would be over.[^practical-fractal-video]
- **Overbought/oversold doesn't exist.** Because price already embeds all current valuations by construction, an oscillator claiming to detect "overbought" is measuring something the market's own definition rules out. He recounts pushback from a tutorial attendee who insisted overbought/oversold must be real "because there's an oscillator for it."[^practical-fractal-video]
- **Fixed-dollar stops mean you're "trading your wallet," not the market** — a stop set from an account-size number (e.g. "$500 on bonds") has no relationship to the market's actual structure.[^practical-fractal-video]
- **Price is always an effect, never a cause** — by the time price moves, momentum and volume have already changed; see [Chaos theory — three principles](/concepts/chaos-theory-three-principles.md) for the fuller causal chain.
