# Trading psychology: "want what the market wants"

Williams states the "Holy Grail" of the talk's title literally: **want what the market wants.** In his framing, all trading frustration comes from holding expectations the market doesn't meet, and the fix is to trade without predicting or expecting any particular outcome.[^practical-fractal-video]

## How he says he evaluates his own trading

Not by P&L, but by two behavioral checks:[^practical-fractal-video]

1. **Speed of execution** — how fast he calls the broker after a valid signal. Any hesitation or rationalizing ("might be a 4th wave...") is, in his words, "a kiss of death."
2. **Indifference to outcome** — whether he can honestly say he doesn't care which way the market goes right now. Caring/rooting for a trade means he's become *reactive* rather than *proactive* — he compares reflexive traders to someone who reacts automatically to being slapped.

He frames genuinely not caring which way the market goes as, on its own, enough to put a trader in the top 3–5% of all traders.[^practical-fractal-video]

## Win rate is a vanity metric

He tells of a newsletter writer (Commodity Traders Consumer Report) who deliberately locked in small wins and let losses run purely to win a "best win/loss ratio" award (67% wins) for the year — and still had a losing year overall. See [Win/loss ratio fallacy](/concepts/win-loss-ratio-fallacy.md) for the fuller argument and his own numbers.[^practical-fractal-video]

## "Give up," and other framing devices

- **"The key to trading is to give up"** — surrendering the need to outsmart or control the market.
- **"Be the tail on the dog, not the nose of the dog"** — attributed as the opposite of trader Tom DeMark's philosophy, which Williams says gets traders into more trouble.
- Rejects financial news/media as an input entirely: no financial TV (jokes CNBC/FNN stands for "Can Never be Correct Financial Neurotic Network," since anchors are paid by the word, not accuracy), no Wall Street Journal, no newsletters — only the chart.
- Risk-aversion, he argues, is *learned* in early childhood before we can reason about it, yet a speculator's actual job is to take on risk others don't want — so **"we don't trade the market, we trade our own personal belief systems."**[^practical-fractal-video]
