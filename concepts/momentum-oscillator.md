# Momentum oscillator (5/34)

The difference between a 5-bar and a 34-bar **simple** (not exponential) moving average, displayed as a histogram. Williams uses it to do Elliott Wave counts, and calls it "as close to infallible as any indicator" he's seen — he says his staff reviewed tens of thousands of charts and found only two errors, each under 0.05 points, on a 10-minute sugar chart and a 15-minute cocoa chart.[^practical-fractal-video]

This is the third of the [five dimensions of market structure](/concepts/five-dimensions-of-market-structure.md), and feeds the [accelerator oscillator](/concepts/accelerator-oscillator.md) (a moving average of this oscillator's value).

## Relationship to MACD

Williams notes that combining this oscillator with the accelerator recreates a standard MACD (the difference is mainly histogram vs. line display) — but stresses that his version must use **simple** moving averages, not the exponential averages in Gerald Appel's original MACD, and claims this choice materially affects profitability.[^practical-fractal-video]

## Elliott Wave application

He states that roughly 85% of "whiplash" losing trades cluster in Elliott wave four, which he explains as foreseeable because acceleration leads momentum, which leads price — see [Chaos theory — three principles](/concepts/chaos-theory-three-principles.md) for the causal chain.[^practical-fractal-video]
